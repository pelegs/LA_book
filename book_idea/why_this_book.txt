Summary:
* Because LA is extremely important* for all sciences, but it is taught horribly: too much abstract things, too many definitions and proofs instead of intuition development, not enough justification for *why* we do things in certain ways, etc.
* My approach: first understand the *why*, then develop the intuition for simple cases (with as much visualization as possible), then generalize - and only then do more rigorous stuff like defs and proofs.
* The book will cover everything science students need to know, including for physics students who arguably need the most topics covered. However, it will be marked what parts are more important for other fields (e.g. biology students generally don't need to learn about dual spaces and functional analysis).

Why this book? Don't we have enough resources already on this fundamental topic?

The direct answer to this question is simple: I really think most resources are doing a bad job of explaining fundamental linear algebra to new students (footnote: and honestly, this isn't restricted to linear algebra but applies to all fundamental mathematical topics - but it's a classic case). Not because they lack material or the complete picture (or even sometimes answering the question *why we do this*) - but because the common approach to the teaching fundamental mathematical topics just doesn't work well for most people (footnote: the main exception is students of pure mathematics and people who can easily deal with extremely abstract concepts - a very specific group of people at the end of the day).
