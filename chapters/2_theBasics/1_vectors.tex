\begin{motivation}{Bouncing/Reflecting Off a Wall}{wall_bounce}
	Imagine we are writing a 3D computer game in which some object is bouncing off a wall. Alternatively, consider 3D graphics in general, where one simulates rays of light reflecting off of smooth surfaces. In both cases the same problem needs to be solved: given a flat plane of some orientation in space, and an object hitting it from some direction - what would be the direction of the object after it bounces off the plane?

	\vspace{1em}
	\begin{center}
		\tdplotsetmaincoords{75}{30}
		\begin{tikzpicture}[tdplot_main_coords, rotate=35]
			\coordinate (a) at (-3,-3,0);
			\coordinate (b) at (3,-3,0);
			\coordinate (c) at (3,3,0);
			\coordinate (d) at (-3,3,0);
			\draw[xpurple, thick, fill=xpurple, opacity=0.2] (a) -- (b) -- (c) -- (d) -- cycle;
			\begin{scope}[canvas is xy plane at z=0]
				\draw[help lines, opacity=0.25] (-3,-3) grid (3,3);
			\end{scope}
			\draw[vector, xred, dashed] (1,1,2) -- (0,0,0);
			\draw[vector, xblue, dashed] (0,0,0) -- (-1,-1,2);
		\end{tikzpicture}
	\end{center}

	\vspace{1em}
	To solve this problem we will need to know how to represent quantities like position, velocity and orientation in 3D, and more generally points, lines and planes - and to know how to calculate the intersection between these different objects. By the end of this section you should be able to solve this problem by yourself, and even generalize it to any number of dimensions.
\end{motivation}

\begin{motivation}{Gravity Force}{gravity_force}
	Consider a simple simulation of classical (\enquote{Newtionian}) gravity, say of a planet with mass $m$ orbiting a star with mass $M$ (where $M\gg m$). At any given moment, the force acting on the planet due to the star's gravity has magnitude
	\begin{equation*}
		F = G\frac{Mm}{r^{2}},
	\end{equation*}
	where $r$ is the distance between the planet and the star at that moment, and $G$ is a universal constant (specifically, $G=\SI{6.674e-11}{\newton\meter\squared\per\kilo\gram\squared}$).

	\vspace{1em}
	However, since we live in 3-dimensional space\footnote{allegedly.}, we also want this force to be pointing in the direction from the planet to the star. To do this, we need to understant how to describe orientations in 3-dimensional space, and be able to pack together both orientation and magnitude in a correct way.
\end{motivation}
